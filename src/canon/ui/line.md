Where a screen's edges fall and what everything on it wears. The `align` and
`look` tools read one capture of the live page and name what disagrees with
itself.

- **Only what is painted is a line.** A box with no background and no border is
  invisible, and lining anything up with it is lining up with nothing.
- **A mark with no box is judged by its middle, and text by its baseline.** A
  chevron and a larger button below it are aligned when their centres agree.
- **A line a few pixels off another is worse than one standing alone.** Standing
  alone reads as deliberate; nearly aligned reads as broken.
- **One kind wears one look, everywhere.** Same lettering, size, weight, case,
  colour, padding and border on every page. A kind is the thing and its state,
  so a caps heading and a sentence-case value are two kinds and are left alone.
- **A value used once is a decision nobody else followed.** Reuse the size, the
  colour and the spacing that exist before inventing one.
- **One state is half a page.** An accordion open and closed, a menu up and
  down, a tab on and off: each is captured and judged on its own.
